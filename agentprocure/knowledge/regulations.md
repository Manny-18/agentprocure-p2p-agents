# Regulatory notes for Procure-to-Pay in India

Teaching summaries as of 10 October 2026, written for this project from the sources listed in the report. They are not legal or tax advice; confirm against the current Act, rules and notifications.

## REG-MSMED MSMED Act, 2006 (Chapter V)

### REG-MSMED-1 Payment deadline for micro and small suppliers
Under section 15 of the MSMED Act, a buyer must pay a micro or small enterprise supplier on the date agreed in writing, which can never be more than 45 days from the day of acceptance of the goods or services. If there is no written agreement, payment is due within 15 days of acceptance. Goods are deemed accepted on delivery if the buyer raises no written objection within 15 days.

### REG-MSMED-2 Interest on late payment
Under section 16, if the buyer pays late it must pay compound interest with monthly rests at three times the Bank Rate notified by the Reserve Bank of India, from the due date, whatever the contract says. Section 17 makes the buyer liable for the principal with this interest.

### REG-MSMED-3 Interest is not tax-deductible
Under section 23, interest paid or payable to a micro or small supplier for delayed payment is not allowed as a deduction when computing taxable income.

### REG-MSMED-4 Who is protected
The delayed-payment protection applies only to a supplier that is a micro or small enterprise registered under Udyam. Medium enterprises are not covered, and retail and wholesale traders registered on Udyam are excluded from these benefits.

### REG-MSMED-5 Recovery route and the 2026 amendment
An unpaid supplier files a reference on the MSME ODR portal, the only route for new references since 15 October 2025, before the Facilitation Council of its own State. The MSMED (Amendment) Act, 2026 received assent on 13 August 2026 and adds fixed timelines and recovery as arrears of land revenue, but its provisions apply only from dates the government notifies; none had been notified by early October 2026. Companies that take more than 45 days to pay such suppliers file a half-yearly MSME Form I return with the Registrar of Companies.

## REG-IT Income-tax Act, 2025

### REG-IT-1 The new Act
The Income-tax Act, 2025 replaced the Income-tax Act, 1961 from 1 April 2026 and uses the term tax year. Earlier years continue to be governed by the 1961 Act. Many section numbers changed, so ERP tax codes, forms and references must be updated.

### REG-IT-2 TDS on purchase of goods
Section 393(1), Table serial 8(ii) (formerly section 194Q) requires a buyer whose turnover exceeded Rs 10 crore in the preceding year to deduct TDS at 0.1% on purchases of goods from a resident seller, only on the amount by which purchases from that seller in the tax year exceed Rs 50 lakh. If the seller has not furnished a PAN the rate is 5%. When tax is deducted at the time of credit and GST is shown separately on the invoice, TDS is computed on the value excluding GST. The provision does not apply where tax is already deductible or collectible on the transaction under another provision. Deductions are reported in the quarterly statement in Form 140.

### REG-IT-3 Deduction only on payment for late MSE dues
Section 37(2)(g) of the Income-tax Act, 2025 (formerly section 43B(h)) allows an amount owed to a micro or small enterprise and paid after the section 15 MSMED deadline to be deducted only in the year it is actually paid. If such a due is still unpaid at the end of the tax year, the expense is disallowed for that year, which raises the tax payable for the year. The Ministry of Finance confirmed this link on 21 July 2026.

## REG-GST Goods and Services Tax

### REG-GST-1 Rate structure after GST 2.0
From 22 September 2025 the main GST rates are 5%, 18% and 40% (plus nil), replacing the old 5%, 12%, 18% and 28% slabs. Most items formerly at 12% moved to 5% or 18%, and most items formerly at 28% moved to 18%, with luxury and sin goods at 40%. The rate for any item must be read from the current rate notification for its HSN code.

### REG-GST-2 IGST or CGST plus SGST
When the supplier's state and the place of supply are the same, the supply is intra-state and attracts CGST and SGST in equal halves. When they differ, the supply is inter-state and attracts IGST at the full rate. Charging the wrong type of tax makes the invoice incorrect and the credit must not be claimed until it is corrected.

### REG-GST-3 GSTIN structure
A GSTIN has 15 characters: a 2-digit state code, the 10-character PAN of the business, an entity number, the letter Z and a check character calculated from the first 14 characters. A GSTIN whose check character does not match is mistyped or fabricated, and a GSTIN whose embedded PAN differs from the supplier's PAN does not belong to that supplier.

### REG-GST-4 E-invoicing and the IRN
E-invoicing is mandatory for a supplier whose aggregate annual turnover exceeded Rs 5 crore in any financial year since 2017-18; its B2B invoices must carry an IRN from the Invoice Registration Portal. Since 1 April 2025, suppliers with turnover of Rs 10 crore or more must report an invoice to the portal within 30 days of its date, after which the portal refuses it. An invoice that should be an e-invoice but has no IRN is not a valid tax invoice for input tax credit.

### REG-GST-5 Conditions for input tax credit
Under section 16(2) of the CGST Act, credit may be taken only when the buyer holds a valid tax invoice addressed to its GSTIN, has received the goods, the invoice is reflected in its GSTR-2B and the tax has been paid by the supplier. If the buyer does not pay the supplier within 180 days of the invoice date, the credit must be reversed until payment is made. A supplier reduces tax already charged by issuing a credit note under section 34.

## REG-RBI Reserve Bank of India rates

### REG-RBI-1 Bank Rate used for MSME interest
On 7 October 2026 the RBI Monetary Policy Committee raised the repo rate to 5.50%; the Marginal Standing Facility rate and the Bank Rate moved to 5.75%. At this Bank Rate, interest on delayed payment to a micro or small enterprise is three times 5.75%, that is 17.25% a year, compounded monthly.
